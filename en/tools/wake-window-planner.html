    What the wake window planner calculates

    Enter your baby's age in whole months (0 to 36) and the planner shows a typical number of naps per day and a typical wake window, which is how long a baby is comfortably awake between sleeps, plus two practical tips for that age.

    It gives a range, not a schedule. Babies of the same age differ a lot, so treat the numbers as a starting point and follow your baby's sleepy cues.

    How it works

    The planner looks up your baby's age in five age bands. These are exactly the values it uses.

    Age (months)Naps per dayWake windowTips shown
    0 to 34-645-90 minKeep short calming wind-downs. Prioritize daytime light exposure.
    4 to 63-41.5-2.5 hProtect first morning nap. Start consistent bedtime cue.
    7 to 112-32-3.5 hAnchor wake-up time. Use 10-20 minute bridge nap if overtired.

    There is no official standard for wake windows. Neither the AAP, the NHS nor the WHO publishes them, so these ranges reflect common practice among pediatric sleep resources. The sources below cover general infant sleep guidance only.

    Frequently asked questions

    How long should a 6-month-old stay awake between naps?

    The planner suggests about 1.5 to 2.5 hours for babies aged 4 to 6 months. It is a typical range, so shorten it if your baby shows tired cues earlier.

    Are wake windows a strict rule?

    No. They are a rule of thumb. Sleepy cues, health, and the day your baby had matter more than the clock.

    Why do wake windows get longer with age?

    Older babies can stay comfortably awake for longer stretches, and they need fewer naps. That is why the nap count in the table falls as the wake window grows.

    Does the planner work for premature babies?

    It uses age in months only. For a premature baby, ask your pediatrician which age to use, as many use corrected age in the first two years.

    Safety first

    This tool is educational and does not diagnose anything. Always put your baby to sleep on their back on a firm, flat surface. Talk to your pediatrician if your baby is hard to wake, very irritable, or has sudden changes in sleep or feeding.
