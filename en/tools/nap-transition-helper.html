    What the nap transition helper checks

    Tell the helper your baby's age in months (0 to 36) and how many naps they take now (1 to 5). It tells you whether a nap drop may be coming, from three naps to two or from two to one, and gives two simple steps to try.

    It is a prompt to watch your baby, not a verdict. Many babies wobble through a phase of short naps or bedtime protests before they are truly ready.

    How it works

    The helper checks three rules in order and shows the first one that matches.

    Rule (in order)Message shownSteps shown
    3 or more naps a day and age 7 months or olderYou may be entering a 3 to 2 nap transition.Hold bedtime earlier for 7-10 days. Watch for shorter morning nap and longer midday nap.
    Exactly 2 naps a day and age 14 months or olderYou may be approaching a 2 to 1 nap transition.Delay nap start by 10-15 minutes every few days. Use quiet time if second nap is refused repeatedly.
    Anything elseCurrent nap count looks age-appropriate.Maintain stable wake-up anchor. Reassess if bedtime resistance lasts over one week.

    There is no official standard for the right age to drop a nap. These ages are common practice, and the sources below cover general infant sleep guidance only.

    Worked example

    Baby aged 10 months taking 3 naps a day. Rule 1 matches (3 naps, 7 months or older), so the tool shows "You may be entering a 3 to 2 nap transition." followed by "Hold bedtime earlier for 7-10 days." and "Watch for shorter morning nap and longer midday nap."

    Frequently asked questions

    When do babies drop from 3 naps to 2?

    Many families see this change in the second half of the first year, but timing varies. The helper starts flagging it from 7 months when your baby still takes 3 or more naps.

    When do babies move from 2 naps to 1?

    Many babies make this move in the second year. The helper starts flagging it from 14 months when your baby takes exactly 2 naps.

    What is a bridge nap?

    A short nap of 10 to 20 minutes that gets an overtired baby to the next sleep. It can help while the schedule is changing.

    How do I know my baby is not ready yet?

    If naps are still easy and bedtime is calm, you can wait. The helper suggests rechecking if bedtime resistance lasts over one week.

    Safety first

    This tool is educational and does not diagnose anything. Keep sleep spaces safe: back to sleep, firm flat surface, nothing loose in the cot. Call your pediatrician if your baby sleeps far more or far less than usual, or seems unwell.
