    What the feeding quantity estimator calculates

    Enter your baby's age (4 to 36 months), weight (3 to 20 kg) and number of meals a day (3 to 7). The estimator shows a rough daily energy target in kcal, an approximate amount per meal, and a typical milk range for their age.

    These are planning numbers to help you picture the day. Babies eat different amounts from day to day, and appetite is a better guide than any total.

    How it works

    Daily energy is your baby's weight in kg multiplied by a number of kcal per kg, rounded to a whole number. The per-meal target is the daily energy divided by the meals per day, rounded.

    Age (months)Energy (kcal per kg a day)Typical milk range
    4 to 882500-700 ml/day
    9 to 1282400-500 ml/day
    13 to 1778400-500 ml/day

    The kcal per kg values and the milk ranges are planning approximations built into this tool. We are not aware of an official table that gives these exact numbers. For official guidance on how often and how much babies eat, see the sources below.

    Worked example

    Baby aged 12 months, 9.5 kg, 5 meals a day. Daily energy is 9.5 x 82 = 779 kcal/day. Per meal is 779 / 5 = 156 kcal/meal. The tool shows "Estimated daily energy: 779 kcal/day", "Approximate per meal target (5 meals): 156 kcal/meal", "Typical milk range by age: 400-500 ml/day" and "Adjust portions to appetite and growth trend with your pediatric team."

    Frequently asked questions

    How many calories does a 1-year-old need?

    The estimator uses 82 kcal per kg for babies up to 12 months and 78 kcal per kg from 13 months. A 9.5 kg baby comes out at 779 kcal a day. Appetite and growth matter more than the exact figure.

    How much milk does a toddler still need?

    The estimator shows 500-700 ml a day for ages 4 to 8 months, 400-500 ml for 9 to 17 months, and 300-450 ml from 18 months, as part of a mix with solid foods.

    Should my baby finish every meal target?

    No. The per-meal number is an average. Let your baby decide how much to eat and watch their growth trend with your pediatrician.

    Does this replace advice from my pediatrician?

    No. If your baby has a medical condition, was born early, or is gaining weight slowly, your pediatrician can give a plan that fits them.

    Safety first

    This tool is educational and does not diagnose anything. Call your pediatrician if your baby refuses feeds for a day, vomits often, gains weight slowly, or shows signs of dehydration such as very few wet diapers.
