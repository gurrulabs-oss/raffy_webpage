    What this tool calculates

    Almost every newborn loses weight in the first days and is back to birth weight by about 10-14 days. Enter your baby's birth weight, today's weight and days of life: the calculator tells you what percentage they have lost (or gained) and compares it with what is expected for that day. For the full explanation, read our guide to newborn weight loss in the first week.

    How it works

    First it calculates (birth weight − today's weight) ÷ birth weight × 100. Then it applies these rules in order and shows the first one that matches:

    RuleWhat it shows
    Lost more than 10%Call the pediatrician: "Call your pediatrician today"
    15 days or more and still below birth weightCall the pediatrician: "Call your pediatrician"
    Lost 8% to 10%, both includedClose to the limit
    Weighs the same as or more than at birthExpected: "Already back to birth weight"
    Lost less than 8%Expected: "Within the expected range"

    Below that, the result repeats what is expected for that day from the guide's day-by-day table. From day 5 on, weight should be going up: with a single measurement the tool cannot tell whether it is still dropping, so compare it with the previous one yourself. For babies older than 4 weeks use the WHO growth percentile calculator, and to keep an eye on hydration, the diaper calculator.

    Worked example

    A baby born at 3,400 g who weighs 3,170 g on day 3 has lost 230 g, or 6.8%. The tool shows "Within the expected range" and reminds you that, for this baby, 10% is 340 g: below 3,060 g, it is worth calling.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much weight is a newborn expected to lose?

    Most newborns lose roughly 5-9% of their birth weight in the first days, and up to about 10% can still be within the normal range. The lowest point is usually around day 3-4. If the loss goes beyond 10%, call your pediatrician.

    When does a newborn get back to birth weight?

    Most babies are back to birth weight by about 10-14 days of life; formula-fed babies often get there a little sooner. If your baby is still below birth weight from day 15 on, tell your pediatrician.

    How is the percentage of weight lost calculated?

    Subtract today's weight from the birth weight, divide by the birth weight and multiply by 100. For example, with 3,400 g at birth and 3,170 g today: (3,400 − 3,170) ÷ 3,400 × 100 = 6.8%.

    Can I enter the weight in kilograms?

    Yes. You can type 3400, 3,400 or 3.4: the calculator reads all three as 3,400 g.

    Does it work for premature babies?

    No. The ranges come from studies of babies born at term. If your baby was born early, their medical team will tell you what loss to expect.

    Safety first

    This tool is educational and does not diagnose anything. Go to the emergency room right now if your baby is limp or lethargic, cannot be woken to feed, has a dry mouth, a sunken soft spot, no wet diaper for many hours, or very yellow skin together with sleepiness or poor feeding.
