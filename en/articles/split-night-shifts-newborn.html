Split night shifts with a newborn: how to share nights and feeds as a couple

How to share the nights and baby care so you both get some unbroken sleep: sample schedules by age, what to do if you breastfeed and when to get help.

In short

Taking shifts does not mean you are both half awake all night: it means each of you gets one unbroken block of sleep while the other is on duty. The simplest way is to split the night in two (for example, 9 pm to 2 am and 2 am to 7 am, times are only a guide). If you breastfeed, your partner can handle everything except the feed, or give one feed of expressed milk once breastfeeding is well established. And writing down feeds and sleeps somewhere you can both see saves the "when did she last eat?" at every handoff.

Why taking shifts works

  • Each of you can sleep one uninterrupted block. With a plan, whoever is off duty sleeps without the baby waking them, and whoever is on duty knows it is their turn. The NHS itself suggests taking turns so you can sleep without being disturbed.
  • The other parent builds their own bond. Feeding, bathing, changing and settling the baby is a way to get to know them and be close to them. Studies link a father or partner being involved in care with a stronger bond with the baby, including when there is no breastfeeding.
  • Less strain on whoever is recovering from the birth. After birth the body needs time to recover, and without a plan almost the whole night tends to fall on the mother.
  • Poor sleep and postnatal depression go hand in hand. Reviews find that sleep problems and postnatal depression are linked, although it is not fully clear which causes which. Protecting both your sleep is a simple way of looking after each other.

How to split the night with a newborn: 3 schedules

There is no single right schedule. Pick the one that fits your jobs and when each of you sleeps best, try it for a week and adjust. The times in the examples are only a guide. What matters is that whoever is off duty can really sleep: another room or earplugs, and the phone on silent.

Sharing the daytime too

Nights weigh the most, but tiredness builds up with everything else. Dividing fixed tasks avoids having to negotiate them every day:

TaskExample of how to share
BathAlways the same person: it becomes their time with the baby.
Bedtime routineOn alternate days, so the baby falls asleep with both of you.
Solid foods (from 6 months)Whoever is not breastfeeding takes care of dinner.
Pediatrician visits and vaccinesWhoever can go; the other reads the notes afterwards.
Home: shopping, dinners, laundryWhoever had the harder night shift does less that day.

If you breastfeed

Breastfeeding does not stop you taking shifts; it changes what each of you does.

  • Everything but the breast: your partner gets up, changes the diaper, brings the baby to bed for the feed, burps them and settles them again. The mother only feeds and carries on sleeping. The NHS sums it up like this: your partner can deal with diapers and dressing the baby in the morning so the mother can go back to sleep.
  • One feed of expressed milk: once breastfeeding is well established, your partner can now and then give one night feed with a bottle so the mother sleeps a longer block. If you are unsure when to start with a bottle, ask your midwife or a lactation consultant.
  • Avoiding overfull breasts: milk supply adapts to what the baby asks for, and if the breast goes many hours without being emptied it can feel very full or uncomfortable. That is why many mothers express a little milk near the time of the feed your partner does, and that same milk is what is given. Expressing also helps relieve an uncomfortably full breast.

If you have doubts, a midwife or a lactation consultant can help you make it fit your baby.

Safety and when to get help

  • Never fall asleep with the baby on a sofa or armchair. The risk of sudden infant death is much higher when a baby sleeps with an adult on a sofa or armchair. If you feel your eyes closing during a feed, put the baby in their crib or wake your partner.
  • If you drive the next day after a very bad night on duty, let the other drive or wait until you are more rested.
  • Ask for help if either of you is sad or hopeless almost every day, enjoys nothing, cannot sleep even when the baby sleeps, or has thoughts of harming yourself or the baby. And it does not only happen to mothers: in studies, around 10% of fathers had depression during pregnancy or the baby's first year, and it is more common around 3-6 months.
  • Who to call: your midwife, GP or pediatrician. In the UK, NHS 111 can help if you need medical help now, and Samaritans (116 123, free, any time) listen if you are struggling or have thoughts of harming yourself. In an emergency, call 999.

Frequently asked questions

How do you split the nights with a newborn?

The simplest way is to split the night into two stretches of about 5 hours (for example, 9 pm to 2 am and 2 am to 7 am). Whoever is on duty handles all the wakings in their stretch and the other sleeps without interruption.

Can a partner take shifts if I breastfeed?

Yes. They can handle everything except the feed (changing, burping, settling back to sleep) or, once breastfeeding is well established, give one feed of expressed milk. In that case it helps if the mother expresses a little milk near that time so her breasts do not get uncomfortably full.

Is it better to alternate nights or split each night?

With a newborn it usually works better to split each night, because nobody can manage a whole night of frequent feeds. When the baby wakes less, alternate nights let each of you sleep a full night.

How do we know what happened on the other's shift?

By writing down every feed and every sleep somewhere you can both see, like a shared tracking app. That way the handoff does not depend on what someone half asleep remembers.

What if one of us works outside the home?

The one who works can take the first stretch of the night and sleep straight through the second half, and make up for it with more tasks in the evening and at weekends. What matters is that you decide the split together and review it every few weeks.
