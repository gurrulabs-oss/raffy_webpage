Nutritional foundations at 8 months

  • Estimated daily energy: around 600-750 kcal/day (depending on size, appetite and milk intake). WHO puts average needs at about 615 kcal/day for ages 6-8 months, but individual babies vary.
  • Approximate macro distribution in this menu: carbs 45-52%, fat 34-39%, protein 12-16% of energy.
  • Milk: breast milk or formula remains the foundation, typically about 600-700 ml/day. This menu uses 600 ml. Figures use standard infant formula values (about 67 kcal per 100 ml); breast milk is similar.
  • Meals: WHO suggests 2-3 meals a day plus milk at 6-8 months. This menu has three meals and an afternoon snack; drop the snack if your baby is not hungry for it.
  • Textures: mashed, minced and lumpy foods, plus soft finger foods such as steamed sticks and ripe fruit. Your baby starts to feed themselves; offer water in an open cup with meals.
  • Iron: iron-rich foods appear most days (meat, fish, egg, lentils, beans, chickpeas, tofu), because iron needs stay high at this age.
  • Allergens: keep offering foods your baby has already tried, and add any new allergen on its own, in a small amount and early in the day. The menu includes egg, fish, yogurt, tofu and a little peanut butter; skip any not yet introduced.
  • Amounts: food weights are cooked, ready-to-eat amounts, estimated from USDA FoodData Central values with no added salt or sugar. Your portions will differ.

From 8 to 9 months: what changes

Over the next weeks, feeding usually evolves in these ways:

  • 1Textures: from mashed and minced foods to chopped soft pieces and more finger foods.
  • 2Meals: three meals plus one or two snacks a day (WHO suggests 3-4 meals plus 1-2 snacks from 9 months).
  • 3Self-feeding: more practice with fingers, the spoon and an open cup.
  • 4Milk: still part of the day, often around 500-600 ml as solids take a bigger share.

Safety notes for complementary feeding

Pediatric safety

  • Always stay with your baby while they eat, seated upright. Gagging is common while learning and is different from choking; consider an infant first-aid class.
  • Finger foods should be soft cooked sticks or ripe fruit that squash easily between your fingers. Avoid whole nuts, whole grapes, popcorn and hard raw pieces of apple or carrot.
  • Avoid honey, added sugar and added salt in the first year, and do not give cow's milk as a drink before 12 months (it is fine in cooking and yogurt).
  • Check fish carefully for bones, and choose fish low in mercury, such as cod, hake or salmon. Avoid swordfish, shark, marlin, king mackerel and tilefish.
  • Offer new allergens one at a time. If your baby has severe eczema or a known food allergy, ask your pediatrician before offering egg or peanut.
  • Call your pediatrician if you see hives, swelling or repeated vomiting after a food (call emergency services for trouble breathing), or if your baby refuses food, loses weight, or was born preterm.

Frequently asked questions

How much should an 8-month-old baby eat in a day?

Most 8-month-olds have 3 meals a day plus an optional snack, with breast milk or formula still the foundation. In total that is usually around 600-750 kcal a day, depending on weight and appetite.

How many meals a day for an 8-month-old baby?

Three meals a day plus an optional snack is common at 8 months, with milk feeds in between. WHO suggests 2-3 meals a day plus milk at 6-8 months and 3-4 meals plus 1-2 snacks from 9 months.

How much milk does an 8-month-old baby need?

Many babies drink around 600-700 ml a day of breast milk or formula at 8 months, adjusted to appetite and pediatric advice. This menu uses 600 ml.

What is a sample menu for baby-led weaning at 8 months?

The same foods can be offered as soft finger-size sticks instead of mash, for example steamed carrot or sweet potato, ripe avocado, soft pear or banana, and strips of soft omelet. Mix both styles if you like, and always supervise.

What finger foods can an 8-month-old eat?

Soft cooked vegetable sticks, ripe fruit, strips of omelet, small pieces of tofu, and pasta shapes your baby can pick up. Skip whole nuts, whole grapes and hard raw pieces.

What menu should I follow after 8 months?

At 9 months, try the 9-month menu, with chopped soft foods and 1-2 snacks. After the first birthday, use the weekly meal planner for 12-23 months for a fresh 7-day menu with milk and calorie checks.
