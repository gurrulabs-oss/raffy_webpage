Nutritional foundations at 11 months

  • Estimated daily energy: around 650-800 kcal/day. WHO estimates average total needs of about 686 kcal/day at 9-11 months, with complementary foods covering about 300 kcal of that for a breastfed baby. Individual needs vary with weight, sex and appetite (EFSA).
  • Meals: WHO suggests 3-4 meals plus 1-2 snacks from 9 months on, alongside milk. This menu has 3 meals, 1-2 snacks and 3 milk feeds.
  • Approximate macro distribution: in this menu, carbs 41-50%, fat 35-45%, protein 14-16% of energy. Single days vary; the week as a whole is what counts.
  • Milk: breast milk or formula often still gives close to half of the energy: usually about 400-600 ml/day, and less as solid food grows. This menu counts 3 × 180 ml.
  • Textures: soft food chopped into small pieces, plus soft finger foods. Many babies are practicing the cup and the spoon now, so let them join in.
  • Iron: include an iron-rich food most days: meat, fish, egg, lentils, beans, chickpeas or tofu, with vitamin C-rich fruit or vegetables on the side.
  • How the numbers work: figures use standard infant formula values (about 67 kcal per 100 ml); breast milk is similar, so the calorie totals hold either way; protein is a little lower with breast milk. Foods are plain, cooked and unsalted, measured in grams (ml for milk and olive oil), with values from USDA FoodData Central rounded to the nearest 5 kcal and whole grams. Treat them as estimates.

From 11 to 12 months: what changes

The first birthday is close. Here is what usually changes in the next weeks:

  • 1Textures: from small chopped pieces to larger soft bites, with family food cut into safe shapes.
  • 2Family food: the same meals you eat at home, with no added salt or sugar.
  • 3Milk: after 12 months, pasteurized whole cow's milk can become the main drink, or breastfeeding can continue. Milk stays part of the day, but meals matter more.
  • 4Portions: they follow your baby's appetite; the energy ranges in this guide remain approximate.

Safety notes for complementary feeding

Pediatric safety

  • Sit your baby upright and stay with them while they eat. Offer soft finger foods, and cut grapes and cherry tomatoes into quarters and halve or squash blueberries. Avoid whole nuts (NHS: not under 5), popcorn and hard raw pieces of apple or carrot.
  • Avoid honey until after the first birthday, and add no salt or sugar to your baby's food. Choose lower-salt bread and cheese and use them in small amounts.
  • This menu includes egg and a little peanut butter in well-cooked or thinned forms. Introduce one new allergen at a time, early in the day, and watch for reactions. If your baby has eczema or a known food allergy, ask your pediatrician first.
  • Choose low-mercury fish such as salmon and cod, and check for bones. Avoid shark, swordfish and marlin (NHS), as well as other high-mercury fish such as king mackerel and tilefish.
  • Cow's milk is fine in cooking and as plain yogurt, but not as the main drink before 12 months.
  • Talk to your pediatrician if your baby coughs or gags a lot, refuses most foods, has poor weight gain, vomits often or shows signs of dehydration. If your baby was born preterm, personalize the plan with your pediatric team. Hives, swelling or breathing trouble after a food need urgent care.
Editorial note: This menu is a practical reference, not a diagnosis or an individual medical prescription.

Frequently asked questions

How many meals a day does an 11-month-old baby eat?

Most 11-month-olds have 3 meals and 1-2 snacks a day, plus about 3 milk feeds. In total that is usually around 650-800 kcal a day, depending on weight and appetite. WHO suggests 3-4 meals plus 1-2 snacks from 9 months.

How much milk does an 11-month-old baby need?

Many 11-month-olds drink about 400-600 ml/day of breast milk or formula, often less as they eat more solid food. Breastfed babies keep feeding on demand. Check your baby's needs with your pediatrician.

How much should an 11-month-old eat at a meal?

There is no single right amount. The portions in this menu (for example 20-55 g of vegetables or starch and 25-40 g of fish, meat or beans) are examples. Offer a little, let your baby decide how much to eat, and expect big differences from day to day.

Can I use this 11-month menu for baby-led weaning?

Yes. Offer the same foods as soft, finger-size sticks, strips or small pieces, or as mashed textures on a spoon, depending on your baby's skills. Always supervise meals and sit your baby upright.

Can an 11-month-old eat what the family eats?

Often yes, if you set aside their portion before adding salt, sugar or sauces, and cut it into soft, small pieces. Avoid honey and whole nuts, and keep cow's milk for cooking and yogurt until 12 months.

What changes at 12 months?

Whole cow's milk can become the main drink, meals look more like the family's, and a fixed menu gets repetitive. See the 12-month weekly menu for the next step.
