When to Move Your Baby From the Crib to a Bed

There is no fixed date: most children make the switch between 18 months and 3 years, and the clearest sign is that they climb out of the crib.

In short

Most children move from a crib to a bed between 18 months and 3 years. If your child sleeps well and the crib is safe, there is no rush. The strongest reason to switch is that they climb or fall out of the crib, meaning the rail is now too low for their height. Avoid doing it right before a new sibling arrives, a move or another big change.

Signs it's time

  • Climbs or falls out of the crib. This is the most important sign: when a child can get out of the crib alone, it may be time to move to a bed.
  • No longer fits comfortably. If the crib has become too small and they sleep curled up or uncomfortable, the switch makes sense.
  • Starting to manage nights without a diaper. If you are dropping the nighttime diaper, a bed they can get in and out of on their own makes it easier to get to the bathroom.

Age alone is a poor reason to switch: if they sleep well and don't try to get out, the crib is still a safe place and you can wait.

When it's better to wait

  • If a sibling is on the way: don't move them to a bed right before, so they don't feel the baby is taking their place. Better to do it well ahead of time or leave it until later.
  • If you are about to move house, or to start a new room or daycare.
  • If there is another big change at home: it's better not to add one more.

If the switch is for safety because they already climb, don't wait: the priority is to prevent a fall, and you can put the crib mattress on the floor until the bed arrives (see below).

Which bed to choose

  • Low bed with a guard rail. It gives a sense of boundary and looks like what they already know.
  • Mattress on the floor (Montessori bed). It reduces the risk of falling and gives them freedom to get up; it requires a very well prepared room, because the child will be able to move around in it.
  • Convertible crib. It keeps a familiar environment and is often good value, but it has to have been chosen from the start.

How to make the switch

  1. Involve them: let them choose the sheets or a soft toy for their new bed.
  2. Keep the same bedtime routine (bath, story, dim light) at the same time; the routine before sleep should end in their bed.
  3. If you can, put the bed in the same spot in the room where the crib was.
  4. Put a safety gate on the bedroom door, so they can move around the room but not wander the house at night.
  5. If they get up at night, take them back to bed calmly and with few words. Don't reward it by letting them come to yours.
  6. Expect bedtime to be harder or night wakings more frequent at first: it's a normal regression.

Moving to a bed and moving to their own room are different decisions: you can do one without the other. If you also need to organize naps and bedtime, see the baby sleep routine by age.

Room safety

Pediatric safety

  • If they climb, don't wait for the new bed: put the crib mattress on the floor in the meantime.
  • Clear the area of furniture and large toys they could hit if they fall.
  • Anchor dressers, shelves and other furniture that could tip over to the wall if the child climbs them, and fit drawer locks so they can't use them as steps.
  • Use a safety gate on the bedroom door and another at the top of the stairs if you have them.
  • Use cordless curtains and blinds; if they have cords, shorten them or keep them out of reach.
  • Cover outlets with protectors the child cannot easily remove.
  • If the bed is next to a window, the window must have locks or stops.
  • For children under one year, no pillows or bulky bedding; so don't make the switch too early.
  • If sleep problems drag on after the switch, talk to your pediatrician.

Frequently asked questions

At what age should a child move from the crib to a bed?

There is no exact age. Most children make the switch between 18 months and 3 years. What matters is safety and how they sleep: if they climb out or no longer fit, it's time to switch; if they sleep well and don't climb, you can wait.

Is moving to a bed at 18 months too early?

It's within the usual range, but it isn't required. If at that age they sleep well in the crib and don't try to get out, there's no rush. If they climb, safety comes first and it's best to make the switch.

Is it better to get a bed with a guard rail, a mattress on the floor or a convertible crib?

All three work. A low bed with a guard rail gives a sense of boundary; a mattress on the floor reduces the risk of falling but requires a safe room; a convertible crib keeps the same environment, but you have to buy it in advance. Choose the one that fits your room and budget.

What do I do if they get out of bed at night?

Take them back to bed calmly, with few words and without turning on bright lights. Keep the same routine and avoid rewarding the escape, for example by letting them sleep in your bed. It often takes several nights in a row.

Is it normal for them to sleep worse after the switch?

Yes. It's common at first to find bedtime harder or to see more night wakings. If sleep problems last or worry you, talk to their pediatrician.

Is moving to a bed the same as moving to their own room?

No. They are two different decisions. You can switch from crib to bed in the room they already sleep in, and move rooms at another time.
