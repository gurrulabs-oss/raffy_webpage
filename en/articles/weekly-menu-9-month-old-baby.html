In short

At 9 months, most babies have 5 meals a day (breakfast, morning snack, lunch, afternoon snack and dinner), around 700-900 kcal in total, with breast milk or formula as the foundation. This 7-day menu averages ~816 kcal/day.

Nutritional foundations at 9 months

  • Estimated daily energy: around 700-900 kcal/day (depending on weight, appetite, and milk intake).
  • Approximate macro distribution: carbs 45-55%, fats 35-40%, protein 10-15% (about 1.2 g/kg/day).
  • Breast milk or formula remains essential, often around 400-500 ml/day.
  • Textures can be thick purees, mashed foods, or adapted BLW depending on development.

Daily meal details

Monday (~820 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml breast milk or formula.
  • Morning snack: pear puree (100 g) + 10 g ground oats.
  • Lunch: potato, carrot, zucchini, chicken (30 g), and 5 ml extra virgin olive oil.
  • Snack: plain whole yogurt + banana.
  • Dinner: whole-grain cereal porridge with formula.

Tuesday (~790 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml milk.
  • Morning snack: cooked apple + 1 tsp almond butter (100% nuts).
  • Lunch: lentils + rice + mixed vegetables + olive oil.
  • Snack: plain yogurt + mango puree.
  • Dinner: small egg omelet + zucchini/potato puree + olive oil.

Wednesday (~830 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml milk.
  • Morning snack: banana + oats puree.
  • Lunch: hake (30 g), potato, green beans, and olive oil.
  • Snack: plain yogurt + pear.
  • Dinner: vegetable cream + cooked quinoa + olive oil.

Thursday (~800 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml milk.
  • Morning snack: peach puree + cereals.
  • Lunch: beef (30 g), potato, pumpkin, and olive oil.
  • Snack: plain yogurt + banana.
  • Dinner: soft lentil puree + rice + vegetables + olive oil.

Friday (~820 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml milk.
  • Morning snack: apple + oats.
  • Lunch: chicken (30 g), small pasta, vegetables, and olive oil.
  • Snack: plain yogurt + pear.
  • Dinner: French omelet + vegetable puree.

Saturday (~835 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml milk.
  • Morning snack: banana + ground oats.
  • Lunch: salmon (30 g), potato, broccoli, carrot, and olive oil.
  • Snack: plain whole yogurt + pear.
  • Dinner: zucchini/pumpkin/potato cream + cooked whole egg + olive oil.

Sunday (~820 kcal)

  • Breakfast: 200 ml milk.
  • Morning snack: peach or nectarine puree + peanut/almond butter.
  • Lunch: mashed chickpea stew with rice, spinach/chard, tomato (optional), and olive oil.
  • Snack: plain yogurt + strawberries (or kiwi).
  • Dinner: sweet potato + zucchini + turkey puree with olive oil.

From 10 to 12 months: what changes

This menu stays a good starting point until your baby's first birthday. Here is what usually changes over these months:

  • 1Textures: from mashed foods to soft pieces your baby can pick up with their fingers.
  • 2Family food: the same meals you eat at home, with no added salt or sugar and in safe shapes.
  • 3Rhythm: three main meals plus one or two snacks; milk is still part of the day.
  • 4Portions: they follow your baby's appetite; the energy ranges in this guide remain approximate.

Weekly nutritional average

Safety notes for complementary feeding

Pediatric safety

  • Avoid honey, added sugar, and added salt in the first year.
  • Always supervise meals and avoid high choking-risk shapes.
  • Introduce allergens one by one in safe forms and watch for reactions.
  • If your baby was preterm, has growth concerns, or feeding difficulties, personalize the plan with your pediatrician.
Editorial note: This menu is a practical reference, not a diagnosis or an individual medical prescription.

Frequently asked questions

How many meals a day does a 9-month-old baby eat?

Most 9-month-olds have 5 meals a day: breakfast, a morning snack, lunch, an afternoon snack and dinner, with breast milk or formula still as the foundation. In total that is usually around 700-900 kcal a day, depending on weight and appetite.

How much milk does a 9-month-old baby still need?

Milk is still a key source of nutrition at 9 months. Many babies continue around 400-500 ml/day of breast milk or formula, adjusted to appetite and pediatric advice.

Can I use this menu for a 10- or 11-month-old?

Yes, as a starting point. Gradually move on to more texture (soft pieces and finger foods) and bring meals closer to what the family eats, with no added salt or sugar. Portions follow your baby's appetite.

Can this 9-month menu be used with baby-led weaning?

Yes. The same foods can be offered in BLW-safe shapes or mashed textures, depending on oral skills and supervision.

What menu should I follow after 12 months?

After the first birthday, babies eat more and more like the rest of the family. To avoid repeating the same meals, use the weekly meal planner for 12-23 months: it creates a fresh 7-day menu with milk and calorie checks.

When should families call their pediatrician about feeding?

Reach out if there are persistent feeding difficulties, poor weight gain concerns, frequent vomiting, signs of dehydration, or suspected allergic reactions.
